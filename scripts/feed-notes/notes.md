# Verse Feed notes

25 verses, each with a historical, doctrinal and practical note drawn only from Brock's approved sources (see GUIDE.md), the source named on every note. Built from notes.json by build.mjs; edit notes.json, not this file.

In the app the three are headed **The Backstory**, **The Big Truth**, **Live It Today**.

## 1. Genesis 1:1

> In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

- **Historical.** The first words of the Bible. Scofield and Larkin held that it tells of an original creation in “the dateless past,” which divine judgment later left “without form and void” (1:2; Jeremiah 4:23–27). *(Genesis 1, Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917), Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*
- **Doctrinal.** God created all things by His own act, and Christ was its agent: “the worlds were framed by the word of God” (Hebrews 11:3). Larkin thought it “not clear” whether the Father existed first alone, with the Son and the Spirit proceeding from Him. *(Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*
- **Practical.** “All beginnings must begin with God.” Put Him first: the first thought every morning, the first aim of every work. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 2. Psalm 23:1

> The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

- **Historical.** David had kept sheep himself, and “understood both the needs of the sheep and the many cares of a shepherd.” *(Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Doctrinal.** “Jehovah-Ra-ah, the Lord my Shepherd” is one of the names of God that show Him meeting every need of man. Only one whose nature has been renewed may call himself the Lord’s sheep. *(Larkin, Dispensational Truth, Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Practical.** “The flock does not keep the shepherd, but the shepherd keeps the flock.” Trust Him with all, in all, and for all. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 3. Psalm 119:105

> Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.

- **Historical.** Eastern towns had no fixed lamps; each traveller carried his own lantern so as not to stumble in the dark streets. *(Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Doctrinal.** God’s word is given as the believer’s guide, not for great decisions only but “in the acts of daily life.” *(Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Practical.** Read it with a loving heart. We may not keep all we read, “but the water that percolates through a sieve cleanses it.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 4. Psalm 46:10

> Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.

- **Historical.** Probably written when Sennacherib’s army surrounded Jerusalem (2 Kings 18). It was Luther’s favourite psalm. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** The God who “maketh wars to cease” rules the nations and will be exalted in all the earth; Meyer reads the psalm as foretelling the final destruction of Antichrist. *(Spurgeon, The Treasury of David, Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** “Be still, O troubled heart! The God of the nations is your Father.” Your lack only leaves more room for His sufficiency. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 5. Psalm 139:14

> I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.

- **Historical.** A psalm of David on God’s knowledge and presence. The verses around it describe God forming him in his mother’s womb (139:13–16). *(Psalm 139, Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Doctrinal.** God is not only the observer of our inmost being but “the acknowledged lord and possessor” of it, and He cared for us before we could know Him. *(Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Practical.** Meditating on God’s thoughts makes us long to be free of evil; pray with David, “lead me in the way everlasting” (139:24). *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 6. Psalm 34:8

> O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.

- **Historical.** Written after David escaped Abimelech by feigning madness (1 Samuel 21), “not a very creditable incident,” yet he praised God for an undeserved deliverance. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day, Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Doctrinal.** “Faith is the soul’s taste”: God’s goodness is known by trusting Him, and those who trust Him are blessed. *(Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*
- **Practical.** “Some experiences must be enjoyed to be understood.” Make your own trial of the Lord’s goodness. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day, Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)*

## 7. Proverbs 3:5

> Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.

- **Historical.** A father’s counsel to his son (Proverbs 3:1), in a passage Meyer calls “The Results of Trusting Jehovah.” *(Proverbs 3, Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** Scofield ties this trust to Psalm 2:12, “Blessed are all they that put their trust in him”: faith rests on the Lord, not on our own reasoning. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** Notice the repeated “all”: all thine heart, all thy ways. “If you do not see your way, wait till God reveals it.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 8. Isaiah 7:14

> Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.

- **Historical.** Given in the reign of Ahaz, when Syria and Samaria invaded Judah (2 Chronicles 28:5). It was addressed not to Ahaz alone but to the whole “house of David.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day, Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Doctrinal.** A prophecy of the virgin birth of Christ, fulfilled in Matthew 1:21–23; the Messiah’s divine parentage rested on it for centuries. *(Larkin, Dispensational Truth, Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “What greater comfort have we than that Jesus is the companion of our pilgrimage?” Immanuel: God with us. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 9. Isaiah 40:31

> But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.

- **Historical.** Isaiah’s word of comfort to God’s people (40:1). The God who calls every star by name will not fail the soul that clings to Him. *(Isaiah 40, Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** The everlasting God “fainteth not, neither is weary,” and gives power to the faint (40:28–29). Larkin also applied the verse to the saints raised in power, who in the coming Kingdom will serve as God’s messengers, “angel like.” *(Isaiah 40, Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*
- **Practical.** Notice the order: mount up, run, walk. “It is more difficult to walk than to mount!” The everyday plod needs Him most. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 10. Isaiah 41:10

> Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.

- **Historical.** Spoken to Israel, God’s servant nation (41:8). Meyer pictures them as captives in Babylon, poor and “as powerless as a worm” before strong foes. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917), Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** Isaiah names three servants of the Lord: David, Israel and the Messiah. Israel proved a faithless servant, but God will yet restore the nation. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “Heart and flesh might fail, but He would strengthen; difficulties might appear insurmountable, but He would help.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 11. Isaiah 53:5

> But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.

- **Historical.** One of Isaiah’s passages on the Servant of the Lord (52:13–53:12), which Scofield identifies with the Messiah. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Doctrinal.** Christ bore the penalty in our place: “if our iniquities are UPON HIM, they are not UPON US.” The believer’s judgment for sin is past, settled at the Cross. *(Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*
- **Practical.** He suffered for sins not His own. Take Him personally as the One who bore yours: “Do it for yourself!” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 12. Matthew 5:18

> For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.

- **Historical.** From the Sermon on the Mount. By “the law and the prophets” the Lord meant the whole Old Testament (Luke 24:44). *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** Christ was made under the law, kept it perfectly, and fulfilled it by His holy life and sacrificial death. God preserves every jot and tittle of what He gave. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “Nothing could exceed our Lord’s reverence for the oracles of God.” Hold Scripture as He held it. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 13. Matthew 6:33

> But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.

- **Historical.** Spoken to the poor; His talk of patched garments and a labourer’s wage shows He “was habituated to the shifts of the poor.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** The kingdom of God takes in all who willingly submit to God, and it is entered only by the new birth (John 3:3). *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “There is all the difference between foresight and foreboding.” Sow in season, then trust Him for the harvest. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 14. Matthew 11:28

> Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

- **Historical.** Spoken after the cities that saw His mighty works refused to repent (11:20–24): “a pivotal point in the ministry of Jesus.” *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Doctrinal.** The rejected King now offers not the kingdom but rest, to any who know their need; rest is found in coming to Christ Himself. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “The invitation is to commit and submit; to come and to bow under the yoke of the Father’s will.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 15. John 1:1

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

- **Historical.** “Logos,” the Greek for “Word,” answers to “Memra,” the name the Jewish paraphrases of Scripture (the Targums) used for God. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Doctrinal.** Christ is eternal and fully God, the expression of the unseen God: “In the Being, Person, and work of Christ, Deity is told out.” *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “Believing and receiving are the same thing.” Let Christ in, and you may call yourself a child of God. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 16. John 3:16

> For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

- **Historical.** Part of Jesus’ words to Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews who came to Him by night (John 3:1–21). *(John 3)*
- **Doctrinal.** “World” means mankind. “Perish” never means to cease to exist or to be conscious; it is the condition of every non-believer. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** Notice the pairs: God and the Son; loved and gave; the world and whosoever; believe and have; not perish but have life. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 17. John 14:6

> Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

- **Historical.** Spoken to Thomas on the night before the Cross (14:5), as the Lord “neared the end.” *(John 14, Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** There is one way to the Father, Christ Himself. Just before, the Lord promised to come again and receive His own (14:3), His coming for His saints, which Scofield distinguishes from His return in glory. *(Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “To see Jesus was to see the Father.” Look for God in the daily life you share with Christ, not only in thunder and splendour. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 18. Romans 5:8

> But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.

- **Historical.** From Paul’s letter to the believers at Rome (1:7), in the chapter on the blessings of being justified by faith (5:1). *(Romans)*
- **Doctrinal.** Christ died for us while we were still sinners, not after we improved. Our standing in grace is sure, “apart from our feelings or deserts.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** Climb from peace to hope to love (5:1–5). “Let us not be content with the ladder foot.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 19. Romans 6:23

> For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

- **Historical.** It closes Paul’s teaching that believers are no longer servants of sin (6:12–22). *(Romans 6, Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** Death is the wages of sin. Salvation is a free gift, not something bought: “The sinner does not buy Christ, Christ buys him” (1 Corinthians 6:20). *(Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*
- **Practical.** “We belong to the one whom, in a crisis, we obey.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 20. Romans 8:28

> And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.

- **Historical.** Part of Paul’s passage on creation and the saints groaning for their full redemption (8:18–30). *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** All things are working, and working together, for the good of those who love God; the called are carried on through every stage to glory (8:30). *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** “Sorrowful soul, take comfort!” Like the cogs of two wheels turning opposite ways, all things work together. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 21. Romans 12:1

> I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.

- **Historical.** “Therefore” ties this appeal to the whole argument of chapters 1–11. Its language is that of the Old Testament burnt offering. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day, Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*
- **Doctrinal.** The burnt offering was not for sin but a type of the offerer’s consecration to the Lord; “service” here means divine service (Hebrews 9:1). *(Larkin, Dispensational Truth, Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Practical.** “It is easier to die once for God than to live always the surrendered life.” He wants us in the full vigour of our lives. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day, Larkin, Dispensational Truth)*

## 22. 2 Corinthians 5:17

> Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

- **Historical.** Paul, criticised at Corinth, cared only to please his Lord; he was “overmastered by his love of Christ.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** In Christ a person is made new, not merely improved. “A new world had been opened by Christ’s resurrection.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** Live in daily touch with that world of faith, “refusing to be judged by the old standards.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 23. Ephesians 2:10

> For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

- **Historical.** It follows directly on “by grace are ye saved through faith… not of works” (2:8–9). *(Ephesians 2, Scofield Reference Bible notes (1917))*
- **Doctrinal.** Good works do not save; they follow salvation. We are God’s “making,” the sense of the Greek word for workmanship, created for works He planned. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** The good works are already prepared for you. Your part is to walk in them. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 24. Philippians 4:13

> I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.

- **Historical.** Written while Paul was a prisoner at Rome: “A prison, a chain, a meager existence!” Yet he could say he abounded (4:18, 22). *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** The strength is Christ’s, and its setting is contentment in God’s will: Paul had learned to be content in every state (4:11–12). *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** Contentment “is a secret that can only be acquired by our experience of life in the will of God.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*

## 25. Hebrews 11:1

> Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.

- **Historical.** It opens the chapter of faith’s heroes, from Abel through Noah and Abraham. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Doctrinal.** Faith makes us as sure of unseen things, known to us only through God’s word, as of things we can see and touch. *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
- **Practical.** The pilgrims and martyrs were derided for trusting the unseen, “And God never fails such.” *(Meyer, Through the Bible Day by Day)*
